AI Stoplight Framework

Set clear boundaries. Protect student thinking. Turn your learning goal into AI directions students can understand.

Framework default: Red light first. Unless you explicitly permit generative AI, students should not use it for the task. Apply this framework in alignment with your district’s expectations.

The goal determines the light.

Choose a light for a specific task or step—not automatically for an entire subject or assignment type.

🔴 RED · Independent work

Show your own thinking.

No generative AI for the specified task.

Choose this when…

You need evidence of what students can independently explain, write, solve, or create.

Make the boundary clear
  • Identify the exact task or stage where AI is prohibited.
  • Name other resources and supports students may use.
  • Ask for work that reveals their process.

Example: Write and explain a program independently to demonstrate the coding skills you have learned.

🟡 YELLOW · Limited support

Use only the help allowed.

AI is allowed only for the steps the teacher identifies.

Choose this when…

Students remain responsible for the core thinking or creation, with specific AI support.

Make the boundary clear
  • Specify permitted help, such as questions or feedback.
  • Name what AI must not produce or rewrite.
  • Require students to explain how they used the help.

Example: Write your own code; use AI to explain an error message, but not to generate replacement code.

🟢 GREEN · AI collaboration

Direct, evaluate, and revise.

AI may contribute substantially within stated boundaries.

Choose this when…

The goal includes evaluating, testing, or improving AI-supported work.

Make the boundary clear
  • Define what AI can contribute.
  • Require verification and meaningful revision.
  • Ask students to explain decisions and acknowledge AI use.

Example: Generate game code with AI, test it, fix errors, and explain how the final program works.

Red limits generative AI, not all technology or support.

Clarify teacher-approved resources and accessibility supports separately. An assignment’s AI boundary should not silently remove those supports. If a tool includes AI features, specify which features students may use.

Human → AI → Human

Use this process whenever AI is permitted. Green still has boundaries.

1 · Human sets the purpose

Identify the learning goal, permitted tool, and allowed task. Keep personal and confidential information out of prompts.

2 · AI supports the task

Use only the approved tool and permitted features. Keep a useful record of the help received.

3 · Human checks the work

Check claims, sources, and bias. Test results, revise thoughtfully, and explain your contribution.
